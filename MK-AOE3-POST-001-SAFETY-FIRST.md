Industrial sites had a century of visible harm before "Safety First" meant anything. The agent industry is having its century this decade — quietly.

A construction worker gets a helmet, an induction, and the right to stop the entire site. An operator of an AI agent gets a chat window.

The mapping is one-to-one, and the agent industry hasn't absorbed a single line of it:

- Permit to work → authorization before consequential action
- Toolbox talk → pre-execution confirmation naming the exact action
- Stop-work authority → one human anywhere in the chain halts every pending action
- Near-miss reporting → incident histories a regulator can inspect
- Safety officer independent of the line → evidence the operator cannot edit

Industrial safety's founding insight: the worker closest to the hazard holds the authority to stop everything. The agent era has not learned it yet.

The first lab that builds stop-work authority for agents — one signal, any human, every pending action halted, logged independently — will have reinvented Safety First for the machine age.

Every standard drafted from here should be checked against that sentence.
